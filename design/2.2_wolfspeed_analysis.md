# **2 · Reasoning & calculations**

Two loss families are computed at the application point (200 V / 10 A / f_sw): conduction and switching.

## **2.1 · Conduction loss**

Conduction loss is driven by the RMS current, using the hot resistance. For a hard-switched pulse of amplitude I_peak present a fraction D of each period, I_rms = I_peak·√D. Consistent with the study, D = 0.5 is applied identically to all candidates so the comparison isolates R_ds(on).

_I_rms = 10 · √0.5 = 7.07 A_

_P_cond = R_ds(on)@175°C × I_rms² = 0.061 × (7.07)² = 0.061 × 50 = 3.05 W_

## **2.2 · Switching loss**

E_on and E_off are given directly, so no derivation from t_r/t_f is required — only a rescale from the datasheet test point to the application point. Switching energy scales linearly with voltage and current (E ∝ V·I).

**Datasheet test point: 400 V, 17.6 A. Application point: 200 V, 10 A.**

_k = (200/400) × (10/17.6) = 0.5 × 0.568 = 0.284_

_E_on = 57 × 0.284 = 16.2 µJ E_off = 14 × 0.284 = 3.98 µJ_

_E_on + E_off ≈ 20.2 µJ per cycle_

_P_sw = (E_on + E_off) × f_sw_

_Caveat: the current-rescale assumes switching energy is proportional to current at fixed R_g; the datasheet value already sits at 175 °C, the design worst case._

## **2.3 · Which frequency**

P_cond is frequency-independent; P_sw grows linearly with f_sw. Both extremes are reported; design against the 100 kHz worst case.
